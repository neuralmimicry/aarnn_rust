# Drosophila workflow

This document covers two workflows:

- the explorer applied to the designed AARNN Drosophila walking/foraging circuit (G1, VX-001);
- the route for real connectome data.

Neither one reproduces or compresses the biological fly.

## 1. Designed circuit (available now)

**Source.** The designed fly circuit is built by `scripts/build_drosophila_walk_network_json.py`, using the BANC body contract (`sim/content/embodiments/generated/drosophila_banc.json`): 428 sensory channels, 143 hidden cells and 48 motor outputs. Its sensory and output labels carry BANC connectome IDs, so the import exercises exact large-ID handling. The circuit comprises:

- the shared hindbrain (MLR, RS, ARTR), with exploration bouts;
- six-leg half-centre oscillators;
- the touch escape with backward-stepping pivot;
- learned foraging, with pivot steering (`--forage-steer-mode pivot`);
- learned anticipation;
- neuromodulators acting on transmission (DA/5-HT/NA).

**Functional contract.** `examples/explorer/tasks/drosophila_open_loop.toml` is generated by `crates/aarnn-explorer/tests/gen_fly_suite.rs`. It runs from a cold start with frozen plasticity, over 8 conditions:

- walking;
- obstacle left and obstacle right (range 0.3, below touch);
- touch left, touch right and touch front;
- feeding;
- hungry with a left odour cue.

In each condition, the window mean of every one of the 48 motor outputs must stay within 10 % + 0.02 of the intact network. This is an output-equivalence contract for a simulated body; it is not a behavioural claim about flies.

**Profile.** `configs/explorer/drosophila.toml` uses graded spike-equivalent GNATs, because the circuit is mostly graded cells, runs 4 workers and has synapse removal off. It protects the learning substrate (plastic, pre-plastic and modulatory cells), since an open-loop frozen contract cannot show that those cells are dispensable for later learning.

**Closed-loop performance.** The MuJoCo foraging trials (`target/qa/forage/run_touch_trials.sh`) use the repository's existing pipeline (`run_sim.sh`, `aarnn-mujoco`):

- 12 hungry releases after 400 s of care;
- metrics: trials reached, mean time to food, and wall contact.

These trials run outside the explorer, because closed-loop bodies are external simulator capabilities.

### VX-001 (user hypothesis, 26 Nov 2026)

Three networks of the same design (the stride-cut foraging fly with the touch escape, learner on, growth off) are compared, all at AARNN depth tier 1:

| Network | Definition |
|---|---|
| non-neuromodulated | tier 1, `aarnn_neuromod_transmission_enabled = false`, `aarnn_neuromod_env_enabled = false`; the learning gates of the modulatory cells are unchanged |
| neuromodulated | tier 1, neuromodulation on (DA / 5-HT / NA / ACh scale synaptic efficacy) |
| GNAT-optimised neuromodulated | the explorer's candidate, found on the learned state of the neuromodulated network (`derive --preset learned-cap`) under `configs/explorer/drosophila_learned.toml`, with its removal set carried back to the naive network (`derive --lower-like`). Two candidates were run: A, without the escape adaptation declared, and B, with it (section 3) |

Why tier 1: designed circuits are built at tier 0, and tier 0 has no neuromodulated transmission (the runner's depth-0 path is bit-identical with or without the switches). A first VX-001 run at tier 0 therefore compared one network with itself: its GNAT analyses of the "modulated" and "non-modulated" flies were identical to every digit. Tier 1 also adds synaptic filters and short-term depression, so tier-1 results are compared with each other; the tier-0 fly is context only.

Why a learned state: under the 1 s naive, frozen contract (`drosophila_open_loop.toml`) the reduction removed HUNGER (the gate of every learned readout) and BOUT_off (the turn-bout clock). Neither does anything visible before learning or within 1 s. The learned-state contract (`drosophila_learned.toml`) adds hungry-cue-right, sated-cue-left and hungry-cue-fall, and the profile declares the bout clock and the ARTR oscillator out of scope (`protect_cell_types`).

**Hypothesis:** the GNAT-optimised version has greater computation depth across fewer neurons, and performs better. Each part receives its own verdict:

- **Fewer neurons:** hidden cells, synapses and state bytes.
- **Greater depth:** the mean and maximum GNAT causal depth, and depth per participating neuron (`aarnn-explorer depth-report`).
- **Better performance:** closed-loop foraging trials.

Results: see section 3.

## 2. Real connectome data (external)

The explorer does not download data and needs no credentials. A real-data study needs the steps below.

1. **Identify the dataset exactly.** Record whether it is adult or larval, the specimen and sex, the release version (for example FlyWire FAFB v783, or BANC), and its coverage (brain only, or brain and nerve cord), together with its licence and file checksums. Different variants must never be merged under one name.
2. **Convert to `aarnn-explorer-sparse-v1`** (see data-formats.md):
   - neurons keep their original integer IDs, written as JSON integers or strings, never floats;
   - each neuron carries its role, cell type, neurotransmitter-derived sign source and position, with `position_kind = "measured"`;
   - edges carry synapse counts;
   - put `source_files` checksums in `dataset`.
3. **Declare the dynamics.** Supply `cell_types` bio parameters and a weight conversion (`count-sign-scale` with an explicit `scale`), plus an `assumption_version`. Unknown signs stay unknown, and their edges are excluded. Where a parameter is uncertain, run an ensemble of assumption versions. Nothing is inferred from wiring alone.
4. **Bind the I/O.** Map sensory and descending neurons to a simulated body's channels through the existing embodiment contracts. State which body dynamics and missing nervous-system parts the model supplies.
5. **Write the contracts.** Write task suites for the functions to preserve. `aarnn-explorer suite-template` gives a starting output-equivalence template. Biological reference observations, where they exist, are a separate challenge split.
6. **Run the explorer workflow:** import, baseline, analyse, experiment, optimise (compact and resilient profiles), validate on the locked split, then export and verify the bundle.

### Scale limits (measured, not assumed)

- **Import.** The importer is sparse and streaming-friendly (budgets checked before allocation).
- **Execution.** Execution uses the legacy runner's dense single-layer matrices, which need about 8 bytes × H² for H hidden cells. At H = 140 000 that is about 157 GB per matrix, so a whole-brain FlyWire import is structural-only on this backend. Executing at that scale needs a sparse canonical backend (`StableShardExecutor`), whose trace seam does not exist yet. This is reported as an unsupported capability rather than replaced by a substitute model.
- **Circuit-scale work.** Circuit-scale extracts of thousands of cells are executable.

## 3. Results

### VX-001 verdicts (27 Nov 2026)

| Part of the hypothesis | Verdict |
|---|---|
| fewer neurons | **yes, slightly**: 143 → 139 hidden cells (−2.8 %), state −3.4 % |
| greater computation depth | **not supported**: GNAT depth unchanged |
| better performance | **no**: over four runs each (48 releases), candidate A reached food in 18, candidate B in 17 and the neuromodulated original in 17 (every p ≥ 0.79). The candidates perform the same as the original, not better |

The reduction preserves function in fewer cells, as its contract asks. It did not add depth or performance, and the contract does not reward either.

The single-run trials (27 Nov, below) first suggested that B was better (6 of 12 against 4 of 12). Three replicate runs per network removed that difference.

### The two candidates

The search on the learned state removed four cells in each run.

| | Candidate A | Candidate B |
|---|---|---|
| Declared protection (`protect_cell_types`) | `Bout:`, `Hindbrain:ARTR` | `Bout:`, `Hindbrain:ARTR`, `Escape:A` |
| Removed | ESC_A_l, REV_lm_sw, TN_l_3, TN_r_2 | REV_lm_sw, TN_l_1, TN_l_3, TN_r_2 |
| Candidate hash | `3d16156d…` | `07fbf9ff…` |

What the removed cells are:

- **TN_\*:** teacher range cells of the anticipation learning. There are several per side, one per range channel, and they are redundant with each other.
- **REV_lm_sw:** the left middle leg's reverse-swing cell in the pivot.
- **ESC_A_l:** the left escape adaptation. Its 1.5 s time constant is longer than the 1 s touch tasks, so the contract could not see it. That made it an undeclared out-of-contract cell (decision D-014). Candidate B declares it, as the salamander profile does.

Both candidates passed the locked-validation learned-state contract, and both bundles reproduce their validation in a clean process. With the removal set carried back to the naive network, both also pass the naive contract (`experiment`, locked split).

### Candidate C: reduced from the true trained state (28 Nov 2026)

A and B were searched on the learned-cap state, which sets every plastic synapse to the plasticity cap. C was searched on the state that closed-loop learning actually produced.

How C was produced:

1. **Trial.** The neuromodulated fly ran one closed-loop trial: the protocol below, at the original places. It reached food in 4 of 12 releases. With `NM_NODE_SNAPSHOT_ON_EXIT_DIR` set, the cluster worker wrote its network on shutdown.
2. **What learning changed.** All 44 plastic synapses moved upward: mean weight 0.0045 → 0.093, 69 % of the cap (0.136), with 16 at the cap. No other synapse and no input or output weight changed. The learned-cap state is therefore an upper bound on the real one.
3. **Trained network.** The exit snapshot has no connectome labels, so the explorer cannot name its runtime sensory channel `needs_energy`. The trained state was therefore built as the naive network with the trained hidden-to-hidden weights transplanted (`target/qa/vx001d/trained/network_d1_mod_trained.json`). It passes the whole learned-state contract.
4. **Search.** Run with `run_trained_w.sh`: candidate `48e94c31…`, 100 proposals, frontier of 8. It removed **REV_lm_sw, TN_l_1, TN_l_2 and TN_r_2**.
5. **Checks.** C passed locked validation, its bundle reproduces in a clean process, and with the removal set carried back to the naive network it passes the naive contract.

C differs from B only in which redundant left teacher range cell it drops: TN_l_2 instead of TN_l_3. Its resources equal B's (table below). Searching the real trained state did not change the size or the class of the reduction.

### Depth

`depth-report`, learned-state contract, development split, 44 trials each, `effective-delivery-graded-v1`:

| Network | Hidden | GNATs per trial | Mean depth | Max depth | Depth per participating neuron |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| non-neuromodulated, naive | 143 | 132.5 | 1.178 | 3 | 0.369 |
| neuromodulated, naive | 143 | 131.6 | 1.169 | 4 | 0.366 |
| candidate A, naive | 139 | 131.7 | 1.169 | 4 | 0.367 |
| candidate B, naive | 139 | 131.6 | 1.169 | 4 | 0.367 |
| non-neuromodulated, learned | 143 | 130.9 | 1.224 | 4 | 0.372 |
| neuromodulated, learned | 143 | 132.1 | 1.201 | 4 | 0.371 |
| candidate A, learned | 139 | 132.3 | 1.201 | 4 | 0.372 |
| candidate B, learned | 139 | 132.1 | 1.201 | 4 | 0.371 |
| candidate C, naive | 139 | 131.6 | 1.169 | 4 | 0.367 |
| neuromodulated, trained (real learned weights) | 143 | 131.0 | 1.195 | 4 | 0.370 |
| candidate C, trained | 139 | 131.0 | 1.196 | 4 | 0.370 |

- **The reduction leaves depth unchanged.** Mean and maximum depth are identical to three decimals. The removed cells are a redundant teacher relay, a leg reverse cell and an adaptation cell; removing them shortens no measured thread.
- **Depth per neuron rises only by arithmetic.** The ratio of mean depth to hidden cells goes up 2.9 %, solely because there are four fewer cells.
- **Neuromodulation does not deepen threads in these trials.** The non-neuromodulated fly's mean depth is the same as or slightly higher than the neuromodulated fly's.
- **The real trained state gives the same picture.** With the weights closed-loop learning produced, mean depth is 1.195 (the cap approximation gives 1.201), and the 95th-percentile maximum depth is 3 rather than 2. Candidate C leaves it unchanged (1.196).
- **Learning adds depth, by about 0.03-0.05.** The learned state's newly effective pathways lengthen some threads; the non-neuromodulated fly's maximum goes from 3 to 4.

### Performance (closed-loop MuJoCo foraging)

Protocol: `target/qa/vx001d/run_trials.sh`, the same as `run_touch_trials.sh`:

- care until 400 s;
- then 12 hungry releases of 36 s each at the fly's fixed places, starting with an energy reserve of 0.30;
- the brain persists and keeps learning across the releases.

| Network | Reached | 95 % CI | Median time to food | Mean time (a miss counts as 36 s) | Heading error | Tipped windows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| non-neuromodulated, tier 1 | 1/12 | 0.00-0.38 | 29.5 s | 35.5 s | 88° | 0 |
| neuromodulated, tier 1 | 4/12 | 0.10-0.65 | 19.3 s | 30.8 s | 74° | 3 |
| candidate A | 4/12 | 0.10-0.65 | 18.5 s | 31.1 s | 72° | 0 |
| **candidate B** | **6/12** | 0.21-0.79 | 18.8 s | **27.4 s** | **63°** | 2 |
| tier 2, non-neuromodulated / neuromodulated | 1/12 / 5/12 | | | 35.3 / 30.0 s | 90° / 72° | 0 / 1 |
| tier 0 (context: designed default, fly_touch `off`) | 9/12 | 0.43-0.95 | 14.7 s | 21.0 s | | |

How to read the table:

- The 95 % CI is Clopper-Pearson.
- Tipped windows count the releases (of 12) in which the body's up-axis fell below 0.5.
- No tier-1 or tier-2 network touched a wall in more than one of its 12 release windows. The tier-0 fly touched a wall in 3.

Pairwise tests (Fisher exact test on releases reached; Mann-Whitney U test on times, with a miss counted as 36 s):

| Comparison | Fisher p | Mann-Whitney p |
|---|---|---|
| candidate B vs neuromodulated | 0.68 | 0.32 |
| candidate A vs neuromodulated | 1.00 | 0.92 |
| candidate B vs non-neuromodulated | 0.07 | **0.02** |
| neuromodulated vs non-neuromodulated (tier 1) | 0.32 | 0.11 |
| neuromodulated vs non-neuromodulated (tiers 1 and 2 pooled, 9/24 vs 2/24) | **0.036** | |

What the trials show:

- **Neuromodulation helps foraging at tiers 1 and 2** (the replicates below weaken this). The non-neuromodulated flies do not steer: their heading error is about 90°, and each reached food only in its last release. They also tip less and need fewer caretaker feeds (1 against 3), consistent with less vigorous walking.
- **The GNAT candidates keep the neuromodulated fly's foraging.** Candidate A is equal to the original. Candidate B is numerically better on every measure, but no test separates it from the original. The original and B reach food in different releases, so release-to-release variation in this protocol is as large as the difference.
- **Tier 0 does better than every tier-1 network.** The tier-1 synaptic filter and short-term depression were not tuned for this designed circuit. Tier 0 is context, not a contender, because it has no neuromodulated transmission.
- **Limits:** each network ran once (one persisting brain, 12 dependent releases), in simulation, with the cap-based learned state used only for the search. The replicates below address the single run.

### Replicates (28 Nov 2026)

Protocol: `target/qa/vx001d/run_replicates.sh`. Each of the four tier-1 networks ran three more times (seeds 1-3) with the protocol above. Each run had its own persisting brain. Each release place was jittered by up to ±0.03 m in x and in y, and by up to ±0.5 rad in heading. A given seed used the same places for every network. Seed 0 is the original trial. Analysis: `target/qa/vx001d/analyse_replicates.py`, which writes `replicates_summary.json`.

| Network | Releases reached per run (seeds 0, 1, 2, 3) | Total | 95 % CI | Mean time (a miss counts as 36 s) | Median time of those reached | Tipped windows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| non-neuromodulated | 1, 0, 2, 7 | 10/48 | 0.10-0.35 | 32.3 s | 14.3 s | 0 |
| neuromodulated | 4, 7, 4, 2 | 17/48 | 0.22-0.51 | 30.2 s | 18.4 s | 5 |
| candidate A | 4, 5, 4, 5 | 18/48 | 0.24-0.53 | 30.9 s | 22.5 s | 2 |
| candidate B | 6, 5, 3, 3 | 17/48 | 0.22-0.51 | 29.5 s | 15.7 s | 4 |

The tests:

- **CMH:** Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel, stratified by seed, so that each stratum shares its release places.
- **Fisher:** Fisher exact test on releases reached, pooled.
- **Mann-Whitney:** Mann-Whitney U test on the 48 release times.
- **Permutation:** an exact permutation test on the per-run counts. It is the most conservative, because releases within a run share one brain and are not independent.
- **Difference:** the difference in the proportion reached, with a Newcombe 95 % interval.

| Comparison | Difference in proportion reached | CMH p | Fisher p | Mann-Whitney p | Permutation p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| candidate B − neuromodulated | 0.00 [−0.19, +0.19] | 0.83 | 1.00 | 0.82 | 1.00 |
| candidate A − neuromodulated | +0.02 [−0.17, +0.21] | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.96 | 1.00 |
| candidate B − candidate A | −0.02 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.79 | 1.00 |
| neuromodulated − non-neuromodulated | +0.15 [−0.03, +0.31] | 0.18 | 0.17 | 0.16 | 0.46 |
| candidate B − non-neuromodulated | +0.15 | 0.17 | 0.17 | 0.15 | 0.40 |

What the replicates show:

- **The GNAT reductions keep the function and gain nothing.** A and B each match the original to within one release in 48. The intervals rule out a loss larger than about 0.19 in proportion reached, which is about 9 of 48 releases. Tighter equivalence would need more runs.
- **B's single-run lead was noise.** Runs of one network varied from 2 to 7 of 12. The original's best run (7 of 12) beat B's best (6).
- **The neuromodulation advantage weakens.** With replicates, the neuromodulated flies lead by 7 releases in 48 (p ≈ 0.17). The earlier pooled-tier figure (p = 0.036) came from single runs. One non-neuromodulated run (seed 3) reached food in 7 of 12, so steering without neuromodulation is possible but unreliable: the other three runs reached 3 in 36.
- **Run-level variance is the limit.** Between-run variation is as large as any effect seen here. Resolving a difference of about 0.15 in proportion, at 80 % power, would need about 160 releases per network (14 runs) if releases were independent. Because releases within one run share a brain, it would need more.
