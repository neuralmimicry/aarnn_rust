# G2: a new robot from reverse-engineered networks

G2 asked: reverse-engineer several AARNN networks, then use what they share to build a network for a new style of robot. This page reports the new robot, the evidence the explorer gave, and how far the new network can be reduced. Everything here is simulated; nothing was deployed to hardware.

## 1. The new body: `salamander_crawler`

The salamander crawler is a sprawling quadruped. Its trunk bends, and the limbs and the body wave work together, which none of the existing designed bodies does: they swim (fish), walk on six legs (fly, hexapod) or walk without a body wave (legged explorer).

- Generated by `scripts/embodiment.py` with `scripts/embodiment_salamander.py`, into `sim/content/embodiments/generated/salamander_crawler.{xml,json,config.json}`.
- 4 trunk segments with 3 axial yaw joints (neck, trunk, tail); 4 legs, each with a coxa and a lift joint.
- 45 sensors and 22 motor outputs. The sensors are:
  - 11 joint angles;
  - 4 foot contacts;
  - a 3-axis accelerometer and a 3-axis gyroscope;
  - touch front/left/right;
  - 16 range rays and a downward ray;
  - 4 needs channels (energy, consumption, left and right odour cue).
- A scripted gait walks 0.56 m in 5 s. The MuJoCo native test (`adapters/mujoco/tests/native.rs`, now 29 bodies) and the legacy binding test cover it.

## 2. What the explorer found in the existing networks

Four designed networks were imported, run under output-equivalence template contracts (`aarnn-explorer suite-template`), and analysed into GNATs: zebrafish swim, Drosophila walk/forage, hexapod reflex and legged explorer. The salamander was analysed the same way once built. The method was `effective-delivery-graded-v1` (`target/qa/g2/run_g2.sh`, project `target/g2-explorer`).

`aarnn-explorer library` grouped the recurring subthreads by cell role. Roles are cell names with side, leg and index stripped (`role-name-v1`), and are a naming-based homology hypothesis.

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| recurring motifs (verified analogous subthreads) | 42 |
| motifs that recur in more than one network | 0 |
| roles present in two or more networks | 33 (e.g. ARTR in four networks; the leg half-centres A_st / A_sw in fly, legged and salamander) |
| hidden role edges seen in GNATs of two or more networks | 1: E_st -> C_st (fly 1566, salamander 346 occurrences) |

The reading: the networks share **blocks** (named cell classes and their wiring rules), but each body's **recurring causal threads** are its own. What can be reused is the block library, not a thread. So the new network was composed from blocks:

| Block (`scripts/aarnn_circuit.py`) | Taken from |
|---|---|
| hindbrain: MLR command, bistable RS turn decision, ARTR exploration oscillator, turn bouts | fish, fly, hexapod (ARTR is a shared role in four networks) |
| axial swing half-centres E/A/C per joint, descending excitation, crossed inhibition | zebrafish spinal block |
| leg swing/stance half-centres with neighbour inhibition (diagonal trot) | fly, hexapod, legged (A_st / A_sw / E_st / C_st shared roles) |
| girdle coupling: stance half-centres entrain the axial segment on the same side | new (Ijspeert et al. 2007) |
| touch escape (C-bend away, turn through RS) | fish, fly |
| learned foraging with steering (axial bend and stride shortening toward food) | fish, fly |

The builder is `scripts/build_salamander_crawler_network_json.py` (defaults: axial adaptation 300 ms, girdle coupling 6 mV/ms).

## 3. The new network walks

In a 30 s closed-loop MuJoCo walk (`body_walk_xl.sh salamander_crawler`, 15 000 steps, sated), the full network goes 0.85 m net along a 3.40 m path. Its lowest body height stays at 0.015 m: it does not roll over.

## 4. How far it can be reduced

The same network was reduced under three contracts. Each reduced network was validated on the locked split and its bundle reproduced the validation. Each was then walked for 30 s as in section 3.

| Contract | Removed | Hidden | Walk net / path | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 conditions, 1 s, naive frozen | ARTR_r, FALL, RISE, STEER_l, STEER_r | 91 | 1.26 m / 3.55 m | invalid: the learned readouts and half of the exploration oscillator were removed |
| + 20 s exploration task, readouts protected (D-010) | ARTR_r, BOUT_A_off, ESC_A_l, ESC_A_r, FALL, HUNGER, TCH_l_1, TCH_r_2 | 88 | 1.26 m / 3.51 m | invalid: HUNGER (gate of every learned readout), FALL (reads V_SLOW), the bout clock, escape adaptation, and ARTR_r again (the oscillator never switched sides within 20 s) |
| learned state (D-012), slow rhythms declared protected (D-013) | TCH_l_1, TCH_r_2 | 94 | 0.84 m / 3.29 m | **valid**: two redundant touch relays. The untrained network keeps its naive contract, including the 20 s task |

- **Straighter walks were lost exploration.** The two invalid reductions walked farther (1.26 m against 0.85 m). That was not an improvement: they had lost the slow side-switching of the oscillator, so they walked straighter. The valid reduction walks like the full network.
- **Stale configs did not change the walk.** The first two reduced walks reused the full network's config, with 96-cell masks for 88 cells. Re-walked with a config synced from the network's own `net`, the 88-cell result is identical to the digit.
- **Commands:**
  - learned-state run: `target/qa/g2/run_salamander_learned.sh` (log `salamander_learned.log`);
  - bundle: `target/qa/g2/salamander-reduced-learned`;
  - closed-loop network: `target/qa/salamander/network_reduced3.json`.

## 5. Conclusions

- Reverse engineering across the designed networks shows a shared block vocabulary but body-specific causal threads. A new body is built by composing blocks, then its own GNATs are found; threads from other bodies do not carry over.
- Once the contract covers learned and slow function, the composed network is close to minimal: only 2 of 96 hidden cells could be removed. Its blocks carry little dead weight.
- A reduction preserves exactly what its contract tests. A network that learns needs a learned-state contract; function slower than the trials needs a long task or an explicit declaration.
